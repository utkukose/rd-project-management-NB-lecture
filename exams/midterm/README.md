## Overview

The midterm capstone integrates Weeks 1 to 7: research and development concepts, research methods, literature analysis, funding programmes and proposal writing [1, 2, 3, 4]. Each student writes a complete research proposal on a topic in computer science, using either the TÜBİTAK or the NSF structure.

## Formats

The midterm is a complete research proposal on a computer science topic, written in English with the structure of one of two templates. With the TÜBİTAK structure, the proposal follows the current 1001 application form, or the 1002-A form for a shorter project [5, 6]. It has summaries in Turkish and English of up to 600 words each. Its sections cover originality, method, project management with a work plan and a risk plan with alternatives, and wider impact. For 1001, the main text has no more than 25 pages, excluding annexes. With the NSF structure, the proposal contains a one-page Project Summary with an overview and separate statements on intellectual merit and broader impacts. The Project Description that follows is usually limited to 15 pages [7]. In both cases the current official template is authoritative. The weights below are indicative; in active semesters the instructor confirms them at the start of the semester.

## How to work on this capstone

**Step 1. Choose a topic and a structure.** Pick one of the suggested topics below, or continue the project idea of your weekly tasks. Choose the TÜBİTAK or the NSF structure and download the current official template.

**Step 2. Reuse the weekly tasks.** The tasks of Weeks 1 to 7 already produce parts of the proposal:

- Week 1: the project idea
- Week 2: the method
- Week 3: the literature review
- Week 4: the funding plan
- Week 5: the concept note
- Week 6: the objectives, the originality and the work plan
- Week 7: the budget justification

**Step 3. Write the proposal.** Fill every section of the template and keep to its page and word limits.

**Step 4. Add the annexes.** Prepare the budget with its justification from Week 7 and a Gantt chart, for example from Part C of the interactive lab of Week 6. Add a risk register with a plan B for each main risk, as the lecture section "Method and work plan" of Week 6 describes. Finally, write a one-page cover note that names the target programme and explains how the proposal addresses each evaluation criterion.

**Step 5. Check and submit.** Check the proposal against the evaluation criteria of the chosen funder. For the TÜBİTAK structure, Part B of the interactive lab of Week 4 offers a self-check. Send the proposal and its annexes as PDF files. The submission section below gives the deadline for active semesters.

## Suggested research topics

The titles below are starting points. Students may narrow a title or propose a related one that fits the scope of the capstone.

1. Privacy-preserving federated learning for clinical decision support across hospitals
2. Energy-aware scheduling of AI training workloads in university data centres
3. Explainable anomaly detection for industrial control systems in small and medium enterprises
4. Speech recognition for Turkish public services with a fairness evaluation across dialects
5. Formally verified smart contracts for transparent public procurement
6. A digital twin of a campus energy system for forecasting and optimisation
7. Robustness certification of vision models for autonomous agricultural machinery
8. Automated feedback and program repair for introductory programming courses
9. Edge computing pipelines for earthquake early warning
10. Trustworthy retrieval-augmented generation for Turkish legal documents

## Report

The submission contains the proposal in the chosen structure, a budget with a justification that links every item to tasks, a Gantt chart and a risk register. A one-page cover note names the target programme and explains how the proposal addresses each evaluation criterion. The proposal may build on the weekly tasks of Weeks 1 to 7. Sources are cited in square brackets.

The proposal follows the current official template of TÜBİTAK or NSF, not the course report template.

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Weight | What is assessed |
|---|---|---|
| Originality and scientific quality | 25% | A clearly defined gap, verifiable objectives and a convincing position against the literature. |
| Method and feasibility | 25% | Methods that reach every objective, with adequate design, data and evaluation. |
| Project management | 20% | A realistic work plan, risks with alternatives and a budget linked to tasks. |
| Wider impact | 15% | Named users, pathways to impact and measures of progress. |
| Compliance and clarity | 15% | Adherence to the chosen template, page limits and clear writing. |

## Submission

This capstone also supports self-learning and can be completed at any pace. In an active semester, the midterm capstone is sent by e-mail to utkukose@sdu.edu.tr or utkukose@gmail.com. The deadline is 23:53 (Türkiye time) on the Sunday that ends Week 7. The e-mail contains the proposal, its annexes and the cover note as PDF files.
